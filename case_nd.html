Personal / Family Project · UX/UI Design

Project ND

Personal homework assistant for autistic students who get the work done but not the understanding.

Role Designer · Builder Stack Claude Design Status Early Mockup

01 — Why I'm Building This

The Problem

When my brother struggles with homework, asking for help usually means either being handed the answer outright or engaging in a stressful nightly negotiation over parent involvement. Neither method actually teaches the material, leaving him focused on task completion for a grade rather than true comprehension, while exhausting limited parental time.

The Goal

Develop an interactive web app that breaks down pasted assignments question by question. Instead of giving away the answer, the tool uses strategic redirection to build his qualitative reasoning skills—helping him actually learn the underlying concepts while drastically reducing the hours a parent must spend supervising.

02 — How It Works

Homework goes in as pasted text. From there, the app's whole job is pacing and framing — one question, restated plainly, with help that escalates only as needed.

Step 01 — Paste It In

The full assignment goes into a prompt area, no formatting cleanup required.

Step 02 — One at a Time

The app breaks the set apart and surfaces a single question, restated in plain language.

Step 03 — A Hint If He's Stuck

Hints only show up if he asks. Each tier gives away a little more — where to look, then narrowing it down, then walking the first part alongside him — without just handing over the answer.

Step 04 — His Part

He answers in his own words. The app tracks whether both parts of the question got covered, not whether the wording matches anything exactly.

Darker means he stopped earlier on the ladder. Level 4 is a normal place to stop — it means he asked for the whole thing, then read the reason why.

A parent-facing dashboard designed to track weekly progress, highlight areas of friction, and quantify the level of assistance required per question. By surfacing these insights, parents can provide targeted support rather than supervising the entire assignment start-to-finish.

What It Shows

High-level trends rather than a granular play-by-play, empowering parents to know exactly when to intervene without needing to ask.

04 — Going Forward

This prototype serves as a foundational model rather than a final solution. The next phase shifts focus from interface design to generative UX research—specifically investigating how neurodivergent students process multi-step instructions to better inform how the app paces and reframes questions.

What's Next

  • Investigating the cognitive processing of multi-step academic tasks in neurodivergent learners.
  • Validating whether the "hint ladder" pacing aligns with natural problem-solving behaviors.
  • Translating research insights into dynamic question restructuring, moving beyond simply adjusting hint frequency.
